# CV evidence evaluation

## Scoring

Answer: does this CV contain enough concrete evidence to make a credible job-specific version? Score the supplied evidence, not the candidate's worth or assumed ability. Dates, contact details, and years of employment are not quantified results by themselves.

Assign integer component scores within these limits, then add them to produce the integer total out of 100:

| Component | Maximum | What to assess |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Quantified results | 30 | Defensible improvements in time, cost, risk, errors, reliability, adoption, performance, revenue, or other outcomes; numbers connected to results rather than scattered metrics. |
| Accomplishment clarity | 25 | What changed, why it mattered, and the candidate's contribution. |
| Scope and scale | 20 | Users, clients, systems, teams, countries, budgets, process volume, complexity, delivery window, or comparable context. |
| Responsibility versus outcome ratio | 15 | Evidence of consequences rather than a list of assigned duties. |
| Interview defensibility | 10 | Baseline, measurement window, attribution, metric source, and claims the candidate can explain. |

Use low component scores for absent or unclear evidence, intermediate scores for uneven evidence, and high scores for clear, repeated support. These are qualitative anchors, not the app's fallback formula. Explain the reasons; do not invent additional fixed penalties or score caps. Concrete qualitative outcomes can earn accomplishment and defensibility credit even without a number.

| Total | Recommendation |
| --- | --- |
| 0–49 | Improve CV first |
| 50–74 | Proceed with caution |
| 75–100 | Proceed |

Use the 0–100 scale directly: 8.6 out of 10 means 86, but a genuinely low score of 8 out of 100 remains 8. A strong score does not mean every claim is verified or that the CV is ready unchanged.

## Strong evidence and weak activity language

Look for action, object, scale, purpose, and result. Relevant evidence includes time saved, costs or errors reduced, risks closed, reliability or adoption improved, migrations delivered, better decisions enabled, and increased scope or complexity.

Flag repeated activity-only wording such as “responsible for,” “supported,” “participated in,” or “implemented improvements” when it fails to explain consequences. The verb alone does not decide quality. Responsibilities can provide useful context, especially alongside outcomes.

For “Supported cloud migration activities,” ask which applications or workloads were involved, what the person owned, who benefited, and what changed. Do not turn it into a completed migration with an invented application count or incident reduction.

Check claims such as efficient, strategic, motivated, proactive, reliable, fast learner, team player, or strong communicator for nearby proof. Keep supported descriptions; flag reliance on unsupported self-assessment rather than banning adjectives.

## Defensibility and metric recovery

Flag impressive numbers with unclear baselines, calculation methods, periods, or attribution. Distinguish opportunity identified, forecast, avoided cost, annualized estimate, approved savings, and realized savings. Do not silently substitute one for another. A team result may be used if the candidate's contribution is clear.

Select questions relevant to the weak claim:

- What changed because of your work? What was the before and after situation?
- How many users, clients, systems, teams, countries, or processes were affected?
- Did time, cost, risk, errors, delays, or manual effort decrease? Did adoption, performance, reliability, satisfaction, revenue, or visibility increase?
- Who used the deliverable, and which decisions or actions did it support?
- What is the measurement period and source? Is the result observed or estimated?
- What did you personally own, influence, or support? Could you defend that wording in an interview?

For cloud or technology work, ask about workloads, environments, cost, reliability, security, governance, and adoption. For strategy, ask which decision, roadmap, or governance change was accepted and by whom. For AI or automation, establish the use case, users, prototype/production/governance/enablement status, and actual outcome. Use safe, non-confidential context where detailed metrics cannot be shared.

## Presentation and career context

| Check | Guidance |
| --- | --- |
| Contact completeness | Look near the top for name, email, phone, and useful location; LinkedIn or portfolio is optional when relevant. Missing contact details usually merit a low-severity warning unless several essentials are absent. Do not invent details. If the review copy is intentionally anonymized, distinguish redaction from an omission in the application CV. |
| Career progression | Long tenure is not a weakness. Around seven or more years at one employer under a single final title may hide internal roles, promotions, scope changes, team changes, or increased responsibility. Recommend showing confirmed progression; do not invent it. |
| Title and responsibility alignment | Broad or hybrid roles are legitimate. Flag reader confusion where title and bullets point to materially different disciplines. Preserve the official title; use a clearly distinguished truthful scope line, explanatory subtitle, or themed bullets. |
| Tense and timing | Past tense for previous roles and completed achievements, including completed work within a current role. Present tense only for ongoing work. Flag patterns that obscure timing; do not over-penalize isolated tense errors. Apply language-appropriate equivalents. |
| Scanability | Surface strong evidence near the top, make positioning clear, and reduce dense or repetitive bullets. If layout is unavailable, assess only the text's structure and say so. |
| Recent evidence | Warn when recent roles are vague and the strongest relevant proof is old. Older rare or highly relevant evidence can still matter; this is not automatic rejection. |

## Education, privacy, and experience selection

Include education/studies when role-relevant, required, or useful as a credential. Do not add a generic Education section automatically. For experienced candidates, suggest omitting unnecessary graduation years, old courses, and dated details that add little value and may reveal age. Preserve legally or professionally required credentials. Treat date removal as a suggestion and respect the candidate's choice or an explicit application requirement.

Flag unnecessary date of birth or exact age, gender, nationality/citizenship, marital/family status, photo references, and full home address. Prefer city/country or region for location. Keep such details only when explicitly required by the relevant process or chosen by an informed user. Work authorization is optional and useful when requested or when it directly resolves a practical hiring concern; do not add it by default. This is practical privacy guidance, not a legal finding or universal country rule. Do not request sensitive details simply to complete the review.

Compress old or irrelevant roles into earlier experience, selected highlights, or short summaries while preserving highly relevant evidence and truthful chronology. Respect a requested “last five years” or “all experience” selection. Do not interpret older omitted evidence as proof of no experience. Do not double-count overlapping roles when describing years of experience.

## Length and seniority

| Career context | Usual guidance |
| --- | --- |
| Junior / early career | About one page, focused on useful studies, projects, internships, tools, and evidence. |
| Mid-career | One to two pages, prioritizing recent relevance and compressing older material. |
| Senior specialist, manager, consultant | Around two pages, retaining outcomes, leadership scope, scale, and progression. |
| Executive, academic, researcher, public sector, highly credentialed expert | More than two pages can be justified by relevant evidence and process expectations. |

These are guidelines, not automatic penalties. Warn when length buries proof or brevity removes it. Do not infer actual page count from word count alone. Consider role, market, sector, and required format when supplied; do not invent local application rules.
