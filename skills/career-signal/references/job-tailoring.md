# Job fit and reconstruction

Use this reference after reviewing CV evidence and resolving the precheck's continuation choice. Keep evidence quality and job fit as separate judgments.

## Interpret the target

Identify the job's expected outcomes, level, role discipline, and important company signals from the supplied company name/description and job description. Separate explicit requirements from interpretation. Do not infer a company's culture or priorities from its name alone. Do not invent company facts or assume the candidate has tools listed only in the job description.

Use the requested role style, such as consulting, strategy, product, technology, engineering, public service, healthcare, research, operations, sales, or leadership, to prioritize relevant evidence. A style is not permission to change the candidate's discipline or seniority.

## Requirement coverage

For each important screening requirement, record its wording, importance, coverage, source evidence, and practical risk.

| Importance | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Required | An apparent must-have or possible knockout: tool, language level, credential, license, clearance, location, on-site schedule, travel, relocation, authorization, domain, or minimum experience. |
| Preferred | Helpful but not stated as mandatory. |
| Soft signal | A broad expectation or ambiguous preference; do not promote it to a hard requirement. |

| Coverage | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Supported by CV | The supplied CV visibly supports the requirement. |
| Partially supported | Relevant evidence exists but exact depth, level, scope, or requirement is unclear. |
| Missing or unknown | No supplied evidence establishes coverage. This does not prove the candidate lacks the ability or eligibility. |
| Not required | Use only to clarify that a discussed signal is not a job requirement; do not use it to hide missing must-haves. |

Call out missing or partial must-haves as possible screening risks. Do not score preferences as harshly as true must-haves. Do not infer nationality, authorization, language fluency, certifications, clearances, relocation willingness, or location from a name, employer, or unrelated experience. If the user confirms additional evidence, identify that source explicitly; do not describe it as already visible in the original CV.

## Profile Match Score

Assign an integer from 0 to 100 based on supplied CV evidence and confirmed additions against the target role/company context. Explain the strongest matches, partial signals, missing requirements, recent relevance, and screening risks. There is no deterministic weighting formula for this score in this portable skill; do not present one as the app's algorithm.

| Score | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| 85–100 | Strong match |
| 70–84 | Good match |
| 50–69 | Partial match |
| 0–49 | Weak match |

Do not assess personality, interview performance, hidden referrals, salary compatibility, internal competition, or other unsupplied facts. Warn that the company makes the final hiring decision and may consider interviews, timing, priorities, competition, compensation, location, travel, legal requirements, or project demand. This score is not a hiring probability or an ATS test result.

## Positioning and reconstruction

- Explain the role diagnosis, company signals where supported, and the strongest honest candidate positioning.
- Prioritize current/recent relevant scope, results, tools, decisions, and ownership. Preserve relevant older evidence when useful. Carry forward hidden progression, tense, privacy, education, contact, length, and title/scope warnings from the precheck.
- Flag over-positioning: do not turn support into leadership, coordination into architecture, a prototype into production, or partial involvement into product ownership or AI expertise.
- Suggest keywords and ATS-friendly skills only where supported. Keep missing job keywords in the gaps/questions, not in the candidate's skills section. Avoid keyword stuffing and any claim of universal ATS compatibility.
- Provide a professional summary grounded in facts; do not polish unsupported adjectives into stronger assertions.
- Pair selected original bullets with proposed rewrites, the reason, and integrity classification. A safe reframing of “supported reporting” is “Supported reporting for [the confirmed team or audience]” only after the bracketed context is confirmed. Until then, preserve the known wording and ask about scope separately.
- Recommend a structure that surfaces strong evidence, preserves truthful titles and chronology, clarifies hybrid scope, and compresses irrelevant history. Make education/studies conditional on relevance or requirements; retain required credentials and explain any proposed privacy change.
- Give a prioritized action plan and a short recruiter-style interpretation based only on visible signals.

## Integrity classification

Classify material recommendations and rewritten bullets using these labels:

| Label | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Directly supported by CV | The existing source explicitly supports the claim. Identify user-confirmed additions separately when relevant. |
| Reasonable reframing | Wording, order, emphasis, or clarity changes without new factual claims or inflated scope. |
| Needs user confirmation | An additional fact, ambiguous ownership, metric, chronology, or scope needs confirmation. Keep it outside the finished CV until confirmed. |
| Not supported and should not be used | Fabricated, contradicted, or unjustified claims. Exclude from proposed final copy; explain if needed. |

For a new fact supplied by the user but absent from the CV, state “user-confirmed addition” in the explanation rather than falsely claiming it was directly supported by the CV. Use Reasonable reframing for a rewrite grounded in that confirmation and retain the source note.

## Application strategy

Choose one practical recommendation, with a short reason tied to evidence, coverage, recent relevance, and knockout risks:

- Apply now.
- Apply after evidence cleanup.
- Apply only with referral or additional context.
- Probably not worth tailoring unless the user has missing evidence outside the CV.

Do not imply that a referral bypasses mandatory requirements. The strategy is advice about effort and missing context, not a prediction of an offer or an instruction to contact anyone.
